# Daraja

*da·ra·ja* (Swahili, noun): a bridge.

Daraja is a bridge-building game set across fourteen Ugandan crossings, from a
ditch in Kawempe to the Source of the Nile at Jinja. You draw a
bridge on an engineering blueprint. Then the drawing becomes a golden-hour gorge,
and a real vehicle drives across. Members glow as they near their limit, then snap.

## Play

Open **`index.html`** in any modern browser (Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari).
That's it: one file, no install, no server, and it works offline. With an
internet connection it also loads its fonts; without one it falls back to
system fonts. Progress and designs are saved in the browser.

## What's in it

- **Chapter 1, Apprentice (6 crossings).** Each one teaches one idea: a single plank, joints, props, triangles, steel and buckling, then cables. The first two draw a pulsing guide for you to follow.
- **Chapter 2, The Nile and beyond (8 crossings).** Named for real Ugandan rivers, falls and gorges, from Nakivubo Channel to the Source of the Nile.
- **Hints in three steps:** a tip, then an engineer's sketch (faint amber lines to trace over), then the full solution. The Hint button starts to glow after two failed tests.
- **Site engineer's notes.** After every test the game reads what happened and explains it in plain words. For example, which member failed first and why (buckling or tension, with forces in kN), whether the frame can fold (Maxwell's counting rule), and which members did almost no work. A marker shows where the bridge failed first.
- **Field notes:** nine short illustrated lessons, unlocked as you go. They cover tension and compression, sag, load paths, the triangle, buckling, cables, spans, shear, and the engineer's job.
- **14 badges**, from Ribbon Cutter to Beat the Engineer.
- **Sandbox:** any span up to 60 m, river piers, rock towers, any vehicle, any time of day. No budget.
- **Time of day:** each crossing is at dawn, noon, dusk or night. Nights have a moon, fireflies, street lamps and bright headlights.
- **Share:** save a postcard PNG of your bridge, or copy a challenge link that opens your exact design for a friend.
- **Building tools:**
  - Mirror (X) draws and erases on both sides of the span at once.
  - Move (G) drags joints around.
  - Before a test, the game checks that the road reaches the far bank.
- **Watching a test:**
  - Slow motion (S) and the forces view (F).
  - Bullet time on the first failure.
  - A crowd that cheers, and confetti in Uganda's colours, when a vehicle makes it across.

## How to build

1. Drag from an amber anchor, or from any joint, to draw a member. Members snap to a 0.5 m grid.
2. Lay a **road** deck from bank to bank. Only road carries wheels.
3. Brace it with triangles of **timber**, **steel** and **cable**.
4. Press **Test** (Space). Pass within budget to get the council's stamp and up to three stars.

| Key | Action |
|---|---|
| `1`–`4` | Road, timber, steel, cable |
| `G` | Move joints |
| `E` / right-click | Erase |
| `X` | Mirror mode |
| `Ctrl+Z` / `Ctrl+Y` | Undo / redo |
| `H` | Hints |
| `Space` | Test / back to the drawing |
| `S` / `F` / `R` | Slow motion / forces view / replay |
| `M` | Sound on or off |
| `?` | Help |

## Stuck? Solutions

After a failed test, **See solutions** (also in the toolbar once you've struggled
on a level) offers up to two designs for that level:

- **Sturdy:** the most safety margin that fits the budget.
- **Lean:** the cheapest bridge found whose busiest member stays at or below 90%.

`tools/solve.js` finds them. It builds hundreds of truss layouts per level, then
cheapens or deletes members one at a time as long as the bridge still holds.
They are the best designs the search found, not proven optima, so there's room
to beat them. Every published solution is checked in `tests/levels.test.js`: it
must cross with no member failing, within budget.

## The engineering is real (simplified)

- The physics is XPBD (extended position-based dynamics) with 40 substeps per frame. Members are axial springs with real stiffness (EA/L), and every member reports its force in kN.
- Members fail in tension at their strength. In compression they fail at the lesser of crushing and buckling, where buckling capacity falls with 1/L², so long struts are weak.
- Cables only pull.
- Vehicles have damped suspension and load the deck through their wheels.
- `tests/levels.test.js` checks one force against a hand calculation (within 5%). It also proves every level can be crossed within budget with a reference design.

## Develop

The game is written in plain JavaScript with no dependencies.

```
src/engine.js    physics, materials, vehicles
src/levels.js    crossings, chapters, field notes, badges
src/solutions.js generated by tools/solve.js
src/game.js      renderer, input, audio, screens
src/style.css    UI styles
src/shell.html   page skeleton
```

```bash
python3 tools/build.py        # inline src/ into index.html
node tests/levels.test.js     # physics sanity, every level solvable, solutions pass
node tools/solve.js 3         # re-search solutions for level 3 (slow: minutes)
```
